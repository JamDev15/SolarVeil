The case for film

The sun is the largest window in your house.

Unfiltered glass lets in roughly half of the solar energy that hits it. A single layer of film, about the thickness of a business card, changes everything that passes through, and nothing about how the house looks from the street.

Cooler rooms, quieter HVAC

Our ceramic films reject up to 97% of the sun's heat before it touches your interior. West-facing rooms stop baking by mid-afternoon and the thermostat stops chasing the sun.

Fade protection that lasts

99.9% of UV radiation is blocked at the glass. Hardwood, rugs, artwork and upholstery keep the color they were made in, on day one and in year ten.

Screens without hot spots

Glare drops to a fraction of what it was. Watch television, work at the desk, or take a call by the window at noon without squinting or closing the shades.

Daytime privacy, kept view

A subtle mirrored or frosted finish means street-level windows read as private during the day, while you keep the view, the light and the open feel.

Installation anatomy

Five steps, and not one shortcut.

Film is only as good as the install behind it. Most callbacks we see come from dusty glass, over-stretched film and edges cut proud of the frame. We do none of those things.

Step four: squeegee out water and air, edge to edge.

  1. 01

    Measure & inspect

    We laser-measure every pane and read the glass itself: single or dual-pane, tempered or low-E, which way it faces. Those readings decide which film keeps your window warranty intact.

  2. 02

    Protect the room

    Drop cloths under each window, sill tape on painted and stained trim, furniture moved clear. Nothing of yours touches our tools or our water.

  3. 03

    Cut & lay the film

    Film is cut to the pane and applied with a slip solution so it can be positioned to the millimeter. Each panel is pulled straight from a sealed roll from the shop.

  4. 04

    Squeegee & trim

    Water and air are worked out from the center to the edges, then the film is trimmed to a hairline gap inside the frame, so the edge you never notice.

  5. 05

    Cure & walkthrough

    We walk every window with you, flag anything you want re-done, and leave a care card. Full cure takes 5–7 days; cold-weather installs can take up to 30.

Ask a question
How long does a home installation take?

Most homes are finished in a single day. A typical 10–15 pane job takes four to six hours; larger whole-home projects may run into a second morning.

Will film damage my windows or void the manufacturer's warranty?

We check your glass type before recommending a film and only install films approved for it. Our film manufacturers back the glass with their own warranty where a window maker's warranty is affected.

Can I still see out at night?

Yes. Clear and ceramic films barely change the view at any hour. Reflective and frosted films are most private during the day; at night, with lights on inside, you will want shades for full privacy.

How long until the film looks finished?

A little haze or a few water pockets in the first days is normal. Film fully cures in 5–7 days in warm weather and up to 30 days in a Colorado winter.

How do I clean tinted windows afterward?

Wait 30 days, then use a soft cloth or squeegee with a mild, ammonia-free cleaner. Avoid abrasive pads and blades on the film side.

What does a typical project cost?

Pricing depends on the film and total square footage. Most homes land between a few hundred dollars for a single room and a few thousand for a whole house. Your written quote is itemized by window.

Do you work outside Denver?

We cover the Denver metro and the Front Range, from Castle Rock to Boulder and Golden to Aurora. Ask if you are a little further out.
